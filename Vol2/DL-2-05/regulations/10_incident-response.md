# 10. Incident Response Guideline

**Purpose** — Sets how a security incident is reported, judged, contained, and closed, and what is done after it is over.

## Article 1 (What counts as an incident)

An incident is anything that harms, or may yet harm, the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of information, and a near miss is treated in the same way as one that landed. Whether the cause was inside or outside makes no difference.

## Article 2 (Reporting)

**Anyone who finds an incident reports it to the information security team immediately.** Judging the size of it is not the finder's job, and a report is never held back out of uncertainty about whether it truly matters.

The report says what was seen, when it was seen, and what has been done so far. Nothing is deleted or repaired before that report has actually been made, however tempting a quick fix looks.

## Article 3 (First response)

The information security team judges the severity and decides whether to contain it. Containment weighs stopping the spread against keeping the evidence intact for the review afterwards.

Where containment would halt work that cannot be halted, the decision goes to the CISO rather than resting with whoever happens to be on duty at that hour.

## Article 4 (Escalation)

A serious incident goes to the CISO and the chief executive without waiting on the reporting line, and where the law requires notice to an authority, the period the law sets governs rather than the company's own timetable.

## Article 5 (Evidence)

Logs, files, and images are collected and held so they cannot be altered, and who handled the evidence and when is recorded alongside it. Evidence is kept until the review has been closed, even where the incident itself turned out small.

## Article 6 (Recovery)

Recovery begins once the cause is identified and the way back is confirmed, and where the cause is not yet known the reason for recovering anyway is recorded and approved by the CISO.

## Article 7 (Closing and review)

An incident is closed with a record of the cause, of what was done, and of what it cost, and that record keeps what was confirmed apart from what remains unconfirmed, so that a later reader is not misled by it.

What was learned is fed back into the guidelines and into the training, and the change is tracked through to completion rather than left standing as a good intention alone.

## Article 8 (No blame for reporting)

A person who reports in good faith is not disadvantaged for having done so, while failing to report an incident they knew about is itself treated as a breach of these rules.

---

This guideline takes effect on 1 January 2026.
