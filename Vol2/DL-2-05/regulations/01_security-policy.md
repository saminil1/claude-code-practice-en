# 01. Information Security Policy

**Purpose** — Sets the top-level rules for protecting every information asset the company holds, and the responsibilities of management. Every guideline below rests on this policy.

## Article 1 (Purpose and basis)

This policy sets the principles that hold risk to the company's information assets at a manageable level. Meeting what the laws and the signed contracts require is the minimum standard, not the goal.

## Article 2 (Scope)

This policy covers every information asset the company owns or manages, on company equipment or on an outside service. It covers employees, contract and seconded staff, interns, and any processor who reaches company information.

Where a lower guideline conflicts with this policy, this policy prevails, and whoever finds the conflict tells the information security team.

## Article 3 (Security objectives)

The objectives are confidentiality, integrity, and availability. Where they conflict, the judgment turns on the effect on the business and the grounds are recorded. Where personal data is involved, confidentiality comes first.

## Article 4 (Responsibility of management)

The chief executive allocates the budget and people that security requires and receives reports on the results. Each department head is responsible for the information that department handles and reports a breach as soon as it comes to light.

## Article 5 (Appointing the CISO)

The chief executive appoints a Chief Information Security Officer and announces it internally. The CISO leads the security plan and may report a serious risk straight to the chief executive.

**The CISO reviews whether this policy is still adequate once a year and decides whether it needs amending.** The result is recorded, including a conclusion that no amendment is needed.

## Article 6 (Organization and roles)

The information security team assesses risk, designs controls, runs checks, and responds to incidents. Each department names a security contact. A committee chaired by the CISO deliberates on policy, on whether to accept a serious risk, and on how an incident is settled afterwards.

## Article 7 (Risk management)

Risk to information assets is identified, assessed, and answered by reducing, avoiding, transferring, or accepting it. Where a risk is accepted, the grounds and the person responsible are recorded, and the risk is assessed again when circumstances change or a new system is brought in.

## Article 8 (Training)

**Security training is given to all staff once a year.** It covers the main points of this policy, recent incident cases, and how to report a concern.

New joiners are trained within one month of joining. Staff who handle personal data and system administrators receive further training suited to the role.

## Article 9 (Action on a breach)

A breach of this policy may bring disciplinary action under the staff rules, and loss caused by intent or gross negligence may bring separate liability. A voluntary report is taken into account, while knowing of a breach and staying silent is judged by the same standard as the breach itself.

## Article 10 (Publication and amendment)

This policy stays published on the internal board and staff may read it at any time. An amendment is announced with its reason, what changes, and the date it takes effect. The history is kept with the earlier versions so it can be confirmed which rule applied at which time.

This guideline takes effect on 1 January 2026.
