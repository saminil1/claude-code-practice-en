# 11. Business Continuity Guideline

**Purpose** — Sets what is done to keep essential work running when a disruption occurs, and how readiness for it is kept up.

## Article 1 (Essential work)

The work that must not stop is identified, and for each the time it may be interrupted and the data loss that can be borne are set and agreed with its owner. The list is reviewed once a year against whatever the business now depends on.

## Article 2 (The plan)

A continuity plan is written for each piece of essential work, covering who decides, who does what, the order in which things are recovered, and the way of working in the meantime while the systems concerned are still down.

The plan is kept somewhere it can still be reached when the usual systems are down, and a printed copy is held by each of the people who would have to use it on the day.

## Article 3 (The call tree)

**The emergency call tree is refreshed twice a year.** It holds the people who decide, the people who act, and any outside contacts that may be needed at the time, with a second way of reaching each of them in case the first one fails.

## Article 4 (Exercises)

**An emergency exercise is run once a year.** It covers at least one essential piece of work, and the result is recorded along with whatever did not go according to plan, because that is the part actually worth fixing afterwards.

## Article 5 (Alternative arrangements)

Where a system cannot be recovered within the time that was set, the way of working without it is written down, and the people who would have to use it are told that it exists and where they can find it on the day.

## Article 6 (Declaring and standing down)

Who may declare an emergency and who may stand it down are both named in advance, and the declaration and the stand-down are each recorded with the exact time at which they happened.

## Article 7 (After the event)

Once normal working resumes, what happened and what it cost are both recorded, and the plan is amended wherever the event showed it to be wrong or incomplete.

## Article 8 (Suppliers)

Where essential work depends on a supplier, that supplier's own continuity arrangement is confirmed in writing and is referred to in the plan, rather than simply being assumed to be adequate for it.

---

This guideline takes effect on 1 January 2026.
