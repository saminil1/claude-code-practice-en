# The 6 check items for an outsourcing contract

These are the items you match against each contract in the Volume 2 section 6.5
exercise. For each contract you check only **whether the clause is there** and
**its article number**.

| No. | Check item | What you look for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Purpose and scope of the outsourced work | Does it name the work, and how far processing goes for it |
| 2 | Limits on sub-outsourcing | Is there a clause that controls the processor handing the work on again |
| 3 | Security measures for personal information | Are specific measures written — access control, encryption, log retention |
| 4 | Management and supervision of the processor | Are the controller's right to inspect and the inspection cycle set |
| 5 | Liability including damages | Is it set in advance who answers for what if an incident happens |
| 6 | Term and handling at termination | Does it say what happens to the personal information when the contract ends |

## How to mark

    ○   the clause is there
    ×   the clause is not there
    △   the clause is there but the wording is vague (see below)

**Vague wording** is a phrase that places no obligation.

    "takes necessary measures"         — no measure is named
    "may review"                       — a discretion, not a duty
    "set by mutual agreement"          — nothing is fixed in advance

## What this exercise does not do

It goes only as far as **whether the clause exists**. It does not judge whether
the content is enough. That is a legal judgement and belongs to legal review.
This automation goes only as far as preparing the review material.

The required items are set by the relevant law and notices, and they can be
amended. The six items above are an example of check items. Your real criteria
must follow the current law and notices and the review standard of your legal team.
